# Pre-analysis plan, M5: long-run effect of childhood exposure to Bolsa Família (cohort design)

Version: draft v1, 2026-11-11. Status: **not yet tagged**. To be committed and tagged `pap-M5-v1` (and posted to OSF) before any outcome variable is loaded for the analysis sample.

**Written blind.** This plan was written without loading, tabulating or plotting any outcome variable by exposure group, birth cohort or state. The only outcome information used is one pooled marginal for the power calculation: the share of people aged 25-44 in PNAD Contínua 2022 (visit 1) living in households whose per-capita labor income is below the line defined in section 4 (14.1%). Coverage by state (section 3) was computed from treatment variables only (programme receipt questions), with no outcome attached.

Sources this plan builds on: `RESEARCH_PLAN.md` (M5 section, template, threats table), `notes/laguinge_2025.md`, `notes/souza_2019.md`, `output/M4/M4_income_check.md`.

## 0. How this extends Laguinge, Gasparini & Neidhöfer (2024/2025)

They compare adults with high vs low *predicted* childhood BF participation (a binary cut on a propensity score from PNAD 2006), born 1985-1989 vs 1974-1984, in one cross-section (PNAD 2014). They find +0.8 years of schooling and +US$250/month labor income (+43%) for predicted beneficiaries. This plan does not re-run that design. It differs on purpose:

| | Laguinge et al. | This plan |
|---|---|---|
| Variation | Predicted household propensity x born-1985 cut | Birth-state BF coverage x eligible childhood years (dose) |
| Estimand | Effect on predicted beneficiaries (TOT-like, with 25-28% misclassification) | Intent-to-treat per eligible child-year at full coverage |
| Cohorts | 1974-1989 (exposed only as teenagers) | 1978-1997 (exposed from early childhood) |
| Outcome data | PNAD 2014 | Census 2022 (state of birth) |
| Primary outcome | Schooling, labor income | Pre-transfer household per-capita labor-income poverty |
| Age vs cohort | Confounded (one cross-section, exposed = youngest) | Same confound in one census; addressed by a same-age placebo in earlier surveys (F5.1b) and state cohort trends (F5.3) |
| National translation | None | Exploratory (section 9) |

Their schooling effect is used as a benchmark (secondary outcome S1), not re-tested as primary.

## 1. Question and primary hypothesis

**Question.** Are adults who spent more of their childhood eligible for Bolsa Família, in states where the programme covered more children, less likely to be poor *before counting any transfer they receive today*?

**H5.1 (primary).** beta < 0 in equation (1): each additional eligible childhood year at 100% birth-state coverage lowers the probability of living in a household below the poverty line on labor income alone.

Expected sign: negative. Expected size: to be written in "Owner predictions" below before tagging. For reference only (not a prediction): the minimum detectable effect in the central power scenario is 0.48 pp per eligible year at full coverage (section 8).

**Change from `RESEARCH_PLAN.md`.** The plan's M5 section names years of schooling as the primary outcome. This PAP makes pre-transfer poverty primary and schooling secondary, because (a) Laguinge et al. and the wider CCT literature (Molina Millán et al. 2019; Parker & Vogl 2023) already establish schooling gains, so a schooling-primary design would mostly replicate them; (b) the project's stated secondary goal is "less poor as adults, before counting any transfer"; (c) labor-income poverty is measured the same way in Census 2022 and PNAD Contínua, while the Census cannot separate Bolsa Família from pensions (see section 4). H5.2 of the plan (poverty effect smaller in relative terms than the schooling effect) is kept as a secondary comparison.

## 2. Data, versions, sample

**Primary data: IBGE Census 2022, sample questionnaire, controlled-access tier** (gov.br login and signed agreement). It identifies exact age (P0181, also age in months P0190), state of birth (P0490), municipality of birth (P0500), schooling (P0790 years of study, P0760 attainment), habitual gross labor income of all jobs (P1080), and one lumped non-labor item "aposentadoria/pensão/Bolsa Família/BPC/aluguel" (P1100). Layout: `Layout Microdados CD2022 - acesso Controlado.xlsx`, IBGE FTP `Censos/Censo_Demografico_2022/Microdados_e_Areas_de_Ponderacao/Documentacao/` (checked 2026-11-11). Reference date 31 July 2022. Record the exact release, file names and download date in `data/raw/MANIFEST_M5.md`.

**Pre-specified fallback if controlled access is not granted by 2027-03-31:** the public tier of the same files. It has state of birth (P0490) but age only in 5-year bands (P0180). Cohorts then become the four bands 25-29, 30-34, 35-39, 40-44 at the census date, with exposure averaged within band (section 3). Weaker, because F5.1 then rests on two pre-period bands; this will be stated with the results.

**Supporting data (already in the pipeline):** PNAD 2004 and 2006 supplements (coverage, section 3); PNAD 2001-2015 annual files, which ask state of birth (V0502, V5030; confirmed in the 2015 SAS input; confirm for each year at build) and are used for F5.1b; PNAD 2014 mobility supplement (parents' education at age 15) for F5.2.

**Not usable for the primary analysis:** PNAD Contínua. Its annual visit files have no state-of-birth item (checked in the 2024 visit-1 dictionary), so exposure could only be assigned by current state. It is used only for power inputs here, and for an exploratory current-state replication (section 9).

**Sample.**
- Born in Brazil, birth state known (P0490 valid), aged 25-44 on 31 July 2022 (birth cohorts 1978-1997; in the controlled tier, birth year from age in months and the reference date).
- Living in private permanent or improvised households (exclude collective dwellings).
- Excluding household members who are pensioners, domestic workers or their relatives (P0170), as in IBGE's per-capita income and in M4.
- People with labor income missing after IBGE imputation: kept if the census file imputes; otherwise dropped, with the share reported by cohort.
- Weights: census sample weights (P0110 or the controlled-tier weight).

## 3. Treatment and exposure

**Exposure (ITT):** Exposure_cs = EligYears_c x Cov_s.

- **EligYears_c**: number of calendar years 2004-2010 in which a child born in year c was of BF-eligible age under the rules in force: age 0-15 (variable benefit) in every year, plus age 16-17 from 2008 (BVJ). Age in year t = t - c. Values: 0 for cohorts 1978-1988; 1 (1989), 2 (1990), 4 (1991), 6 (1992), 7 (1993-1997).
- **Cov_s**: share of children aged 0-15 in the birth state living in a household that reported receiving the programme, averaged over two survey points: PNAD 2004 (Bolsa Família or any predecessor: Bolsa Escola, Bolsa Alimentação, Auxílio Gás, Cartão Alimentação, PETI) and PNAD 2006 (Bolsa Família). Computed with person weights, among households that answered the receipt question. Range: 0.12 (Rio de Janeiro) to 0.61 (Paraíba); weighted SD 0.165. Saved at build as `data/clean/m5_coverage_state.csv`.
  - Why survey-based: administrative state-year counts (MDS/SAGI "VIS Data", Matriz de Informações Sociais) could not be reached from this environment (the gov.br hosts are blocked). Surveys under-count beneficiaries by 30-35% (TD 2499), roughly proportionally across states, which rescales beta but should not create spurious variation.
  - **Robustness (pre-specified):** Cov_s from MDS administrative counts of beneficiary families in December 2004-2006 divided by the 2000 Census child population, once obtained. If the two coverage measures correlate below 0.8 across states, both results are reported with equal prominence.
- Coverage is fixed at its 2004-2006 level on purpose. Later coverage responds to later state poverty, which is what the outcome measures.
- Birth state, not current state, because people migrate (Census 2022 also gives the share living outside their birth state, reported by cohort).

**Predecessor exposure.** Bolsa Escola (federal, from March 2001, ages 6-15) means cohorts 1985-1988 had some exposure in 2001-2003 that EligYears_c = 0 does not count. They are not used as controls in the pre-trend test (F5.1), and a robustness version counts eligible years from 2001 (ages 6-15 in 2001-2003).

## 4. Outcomes

**Primary outcome (one).** Y = 1 if the person lives in a household whose **per-capita habitual labor income** is at or below **R$228.70 per month** (R$178 at June 2018 prices, the Souza et al. 2019 poverty line, moved to July 2022 with the INPC; `cct.mechanical.deflate_line(178, 2022, 7, (2018, 6))`). Household per-capita labor income = sum of P1080 over household members (excluding pensioners, domestic workers and their relatives) divided by the number of those members.

Why this outcome:
- It is the "pre-transfer" household poverty the project is about. It excludes Bolsa Família / Auxílio Brasil, BPC, pensions and rent, because the 2022 Census lumps them in one item (P1100) and BF cannot be separated from pensions. Any pre-transfer measure built from the Census must exclude all of P1100.
- It is measured identically in PNAD Contínua (VD4019) and PNAD, so results can be compared with M4 and with later surveys.
- It avoids the habitual-vs-effective and imputation issues that M4 found for BF income (`output/M4/M4_income_check.md`).
- A household measure captures the channel the programme targets (family poverty), not only the person's own earnings, which are zero for many women and younger adults.
- Weakness, stated now: it depends on household formation (living with parents, partnering), which the programme could also affect. Secondary outcome S3 (own labor income) and exploratory splits by household position address this.

**Secondary outcomes (family S, Romano-Wolf adjusted).**
- S1: years of schooling (P0790).
- S2: completed upper-secondary education (P0760 at "médio completo" or above).
- S3: own habitual labor income (P1080, in R$, zeros included; also reported as inverse hyperbolic sine, exploratory).
- S4: formal employment: occupied and contributing to social security in the main job (P0940), as a share of the whole cohort (not only of the occupied).
- S5: household per-capita labor-income extreme poverty (line R$89 at June 2018 prices = R$114.35 in July 2022).
- S6: household receives any P1100 income (aposentadoria/pensão/BF/BPC/aluguel). For ages 25-44 this is mostly BF/Auxílio Brasil and BPC; it is the closest analogue to Laguinge et al.'s "current receipt" outcome.

## 5. Primary specification

Equation (1), person i born in year c in state s:

Y_ics = alpha_c + gamma_s + beta x (EligYears_c x Cov_s) + X_i' theta + e_ics

- alpha_c: birth-year fixed effects (20 cohorts). In one cross-section they also absorb age.
- gamma_s: birth-state fixed effects (27).
- X_i: only variables fixed at birth: sex and self-declared colour/race (P0210). No current residence, household size, marital status or region of residence (all possible outcomes; see the threats table).
- Estimation: weighted least squares (linear probability model), census sample weights.
- **Inference:** standard errors clustered by birth state (27 clusters). p-values from the **wild cluster bootstrap** (restricted, Webb six-point weights, 9,999 replications) via `pyfixest`. Reported alongside CR1 SEs.
- Test: one-sided at 5% in the predicted direction (beta < 0) for H5.1; the two-sided p-value is also reported.
- Interpretation: beta is the change in the poverty probability per eligible year at 100% coverage. Reported also as the effect of 7 eligible years in a state at the 90th vs the 10th percentile of coverage.

**Event-study version (for F5.1 and the figure):** replace beta x EligYears_c x Cov_s with sum over cohorts c of beta_c x 1[cohort = c] x Cov_s, base cohort 1984 (17 in 2001: never eligible for Bolsa Escola or BF).

## 6. Falsification tests (all must pass before beta is interpreted causally)

**F5.1 Pre-trend (too-old cohorts).** In the event-study version, the coefficients beta_c for cohorts 1978-1983 (relative to 1984) are jointly zero: wild-cluster-bootstrap Wald test, p > 0.10. These cohorts were 17 or older in 2001 and had no Bolsa Escola or BF eligibility.

**F5.1b Same-age placebo (age vs cohort).** In one census, "exposed" means "younger", so a coverage-specific age profile would look like an effect. Re-estimate equation (1) on adults aged 25-44 in the stacked PNAD 2001-2009 annual files (all born 1957-1984, none exposed), assigning each person the EligYears value that a person *of the same age* has in 2022, birth-state FE, birth-year FE, survey-year FE. Pass: placebo beta not significant at 10% *and* its point estimate smaller in absolute value than half of the main beta. (Survey-year x Cov_s interactions included; outcome built from PNAD V4719, monthly income from all jobs, summed over household members as in the primary outcome.)

**F5.2 Predetermined outcome.** In the PNAD 2014 mobility supplement (retrospective, at age 15), the highest education of either parent is regressed on the same exposure, by state at age 15 where available (else birth state), birth-year and state FE. Pass: p > 0.10 and the 95% CI excludes a shift of 0.3 years of parental schooling per 7 eligible years at full coverage. Cohorts available in the supplement will limit the dose range (Laguinge et al. use 1974-1989); if fewer than three exposed cohorts are available, F5.2 is reported but cannot by itself fail the design.

**F5.3 State cohort trends.** Add birth-state-specific linear cohort trends to equation (1). Pass: beta keeps its sign, and the estimate with trends is at least half the size of the estimate without. Also reported (not pass/fail): region x cohort FE instead of state trends.

## 7. Multiple testing

- One primary outcome, one primary test (H5.1). No adjustment.
- Secondary family S1-S6: **Romano-Wolf** step-down adjusted p-values (wild cluster bootstrap, 9,999 replications, birth-state clusters). All six reported, whatever their significance.
- Falsification tests are reported unadjusted (adjustment would make them easier to pass).
- Every specification run is listed in the results file, including any that is not in this plan (labeled exploratory).

## 8. Power and minimum detectable effect

Inputs from data already in hand (no outcome-by-exposure tabulation):
- Cov_s from PNAD 2004/2006 (section 3): weighted SD 0.165.
- EligYears_c over cohorts 1978-1997: SD 3.06. Residual SD of the exposure after both sets of FE: 0.505.
- Pooled outcome marginal: 14.1% of people aged 25-44 in PNAD Contínua 2022 are in households below the line on labor income alone; 65.5 million people aged 25-44.

Assumptions:
- Census sample of about 6.5 million people aged 25-44 (about a 10% sample; to be replaced by the actual count), spread across 540 birth-state x cohort cells in proportion to each state's 2004 child population.
- The binding noise is not sampling but **cell-level shocks**: state-cohort deviations in poverty unrelated to BF, with SD tau, possibly correlated across adjacent cohorts within a state (AR(1), rho).
- 80% power, two-sided 5% test, CR1 SEs with t(26) critical values; MDE = 2.8 x median simulated SE. Monte Carlo with 400 draws per cell of the grid; code in section 11.

| tau (pp) | rho | Median SE of beta | MDE per eligible year at full coverage | MDE, 7 years at full coverage | MDE, 7 years, 90th vs 10th pct coverage (0.59 vs 0.13) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0 | 0.0011 | 0.31 pp | 2.1 pp | 1.0 pp |
| 0.5 | 0.5 | 0.0012 | 0.34 pp | 2.4 pp | 1.1 pp |
| 1.0 | 0 | 0.0013 | 0.37 pp | 2.6 pp | 1.2 pp |
| **1.0** | **0.5** | **0.0017** | **0.48 pp** | **3.3 pp** | **1.5 pp** |
| 2.0 | 0 | 0.0020 | 0.57 pp | 4.0 pp | 1.9 pp |
| 2.0 | 0.5 | 0.0029 | 0.82 pp | 5.8 pp | 2.7 pp |

Central case in bold: the design detects a 3.3 pp lower labor-income poverty rate (about a quarter of the 14.1% base) for a child exposed for 7 years at full coverage. Read against Laguinge et al.'s +10% household per-capita income for predicted beneficiaries exposed only as teenagers (2024 version), effects of this size are plausible but at the upper end. **Power is adequate only if state-cohort shocks are about 1 pp or smaller.** CR1 rejected a true null in 6-15% of draws, which is why inference uses the wild cluster bootstrap.

Pre-specified ex post check: after tagging, tau and rho are estimated from the unexposed cohorts (1978-1984) only, and the MDE is recomputed and reported with the result. A non-significant beta with an ex post MDE above 4 pp (7 years, full coverage) is reported as "uninformative", not as "no effect".

Fallback (public tier, 5-year bands): same inputs give a similar MDE per eligible year (about 0.27 pp at tau = 1, rho = 0, analytic), but F5.1 rests on two bands.

## 9. Exploratory (labeled as such in all outputs)

- Heterogeneity by sex and by colour/race (Laguinge et al. find no income gain for Afro-Brazilians despite schooling gains).
- Exposure counted from 2001 (Bolsa Escola), and coverage averaged 2004-2010.
- Municipality-of-birth exposure (controlled tier, P0500) with municipal BF coverage: overlaps with M6, reported there if M6 starts.
- A Laguinge-style comparison in our data: born 1985+ x top-tercile Cov_s, to show how much of the gap between the two designs is due to estimand and cohorts.
- Current-state replication in PNAD Contínua 2016-2025 (multiple survey years, so age and cohort can be separated, at the cost of migration bias).
- National translation: implied change in the national pre-transfer poverty rate from beta x the national distribution of exposure (as in M2).
- Own labor income as IHS; formal employment among the occupied.

## 10. What would change my mind, and what will be reported if the main test fails

- **If F5.1 or F5.1b fails:** no causal claim. Report the descriptive gradient, the failing test, and the event-study figure (this is the kill criterion in `RESEARCH_PLAN.md`).
- **If F5.3 fails** (effect halves or flips with state trends): report that the result cannot be told apart from state convergence, which Parker & Vogl found hard to separate for men in Mexico.
- **If all tests pass and beta is near zero with a small ex post MDE:** that is a finding. It would say that the schooling gains in Laguinge et al. do not show up as lower household poverty before transfers by ages 25-44, at least not through birth-state variation.
- **If beta < 0 and tests pass:** report it next to Laguinge et al. with the difference in estimand made explicit, and carry it into the M8 national translation.
- In every case, all six secondary outcomes and all falsification results are reported.

Kill criteria (from `RESEARCH_PLAN.md`, made concrete):
1. F5.1 fails: stop causal interpretation; descriptive only.
2. Census 2022 not available in either tier: M5 stops; the exploratory PNAD Contínua current-state version may be reported only as exploratory.
3. Coverage measures disagree (survey vs administrative correlation below 0.8) and give opposite-signed beta: no causal claim.

## 11. Power code (run before tagging; no outcome by exposure)

The steps used, kept here so the numbers can be rebuilt (they read only the parquet files already in `data/clean/ibge/`):

1. Coverage by state: PNAD 2004 (`V2005` = 1, or any predecessor item) and 2006 (`V2403` = 1) households joined to person files; weighted share of members aged 0-15 in recipient households, by `UF`; Cov_s = mean of the 2004 "any programme" share and the 2006 BF share.
2. EligYears_c as defined in section 3.
3. Outcome marginal: PNAD Contínua 2022 visit 1, members with `V2005` 1-16, household sum of `VD4019` x `CO1` divided by members, at or below `deflate_line(178, 2022, None, (2018, 6))`, among `V2009` 25-44, weighted by `V1032`.
4. Monte Carlo: cell-level WLS of a simulated outcome on exposure with state and cohort FE, cell weights = Census sample size, CR1 by state, 400 draws per (tau, rho, beta).

These will be moved into `analysis/m5_00_power.py` when the M5 analysis scripts are created, before tagging.
